Thickness is measured across the stroke: a ray is cast inward from the edge until it leaves the material.
'thinnest' is the worst reading in that area and is the number that decides whether it snaps; 'typical' is the middle reading for the same area, so a lone freak reading is visible as one.
Readings that cross a wedge rather than a stroke — a serif tip, an apex, the end of a swash — are dropped, because thickening the font would not fix them.
